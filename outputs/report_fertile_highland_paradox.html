01

What the estimates say

Stunting prevalence was estimated for every commune in Madagascar, including the 1,173 that contain no DHS cluster at all.

The design-based national figure is 39.3% (SE 0.94 points, design effect 2.12), matching the published EDSMD-V result. The model-implied national figure, aggregating the fitted surface over under-5 population, is 40.7%. Across communes the estimates span 19.7% to 64.1%, with a median of 40.3%.

40% of Madagascar's under-five population lives above 800 m, so this is a contrast between two large groups, not a small subgroup against the rest. The six worst communes are all in Vakinankaratra and Itasy, on the Ankaratra massif: Sabotsy Namatoana tops the list at 64.1% (95% CrI 54.5–72.2).

02

Why this needs a model

The 2021 DHS was powered for 23 reporting regions, not for 1,695 communes. Its 647 clusters touch 528 communes — 31%. The remaining 69% contain no observation whatsoever, and even sampled districts are thin: the median district holds 5 clusters, and 20 hold fewer than 3.

Two models carry the estimation. A BYM2 smoothed-direct model in SUMMER borrows strength across the district adjacency graph. A binomial-family geostatistical model with a Matérn field, fitted by INLA's SPDE approximation on cluster coordinates plus earth-observation covariates, produces a 5 km surface that is then aggregated to communes on WorldPop under-5 counts — over 500 posterior draws, not over the posterior mean, so neighbouring cells' correlated errors do not falsely cancel.

The fitted spatial field has a practical range of 188 km. That is the scale at which stunting risk in Madagascar actually varies, and it is why a purely local reading of any single commune is unwise.

03

The paradox, decomposed

Four hypotheses were pre-specified. Each is a block of covariates entered into a spatial model for cluster mean HAZ, and the quantity watched is one number: the coefficient on highland, defined as elevation above 800 m.

Unadjusted, highland clusters sit 0.209 HAZ SD below lowland ones (95% CrI −0.361 to −0.056). Every model carries the SPDE field, so this is a penalty over and above smooth spatial structure — not a relabelled "central Madagascar" indicator.

Each hypothesis on its own

Entered cumulatively, blocks are credited in the order they arrive. Entered alone, each shows what it can carry by itself — and two of the four move the penalty the wrong way.

Three readings to carry forward

Altitude dominates, but cannot be separated from cold. H1 accounts for 41% alone. Elevation correlates −0.90 with the coldest-month minimum and −0.93 with mean temperature, so this is a single altitude–temperature construct. It is not evidence for a specifically thermal mechanism over a hypoxic or respiratory one.

Diet only works conditional on altitude. Alone, H2 widens the gap by 13.5%. Added after H1 it takes the total to 59%, the maximum reached. Dietary diversity matters once altitude is held constant, not on its own.

The highlands are not the deprived periphery. They have half the travel time to cities (200 vs 390 min), shorter trips to health facilities (55 vs 81 min), twelve times the night-light intensity, more cattle (2,184 vs 1,531 head per 10 km cell) and 3.9× the cropland. The paradox is sharper than a poverty story allows.

04

Against the two precedents

The World Bank's 2023 commune-level SAE (WP 10627) mapped the same pattern but reported a stunting model with marginal R² near 0.075, and stated that it could not explain the highland concentration. Fitting a Fay–Herriot model on household-type covariates alone reproduces that weakness starkly; adding earth-observation covariates does not.

The household arm here is built from district means of wealth, maternal education and urban share taken from the same survey as the response, whereas the World Bank's came from an independent census. Same-sample covariates flatter a baseline; this one still explains essentially nothing.

The BYM2 model apportions 30.5% of residual district variation to spatially structured effects (φ = 0.305, 95% CrI 0.021–0.793) — a wide interval, and a reminder that at 119 sampled districts the split between structured and unstructured variation is only weakly identified.

Two signs point the wrong way

open_defecation is associated with higher HAZ and improved_water with lower, both conditional on the spatial field and 25 other terms. These are almost certainly confounded rather than causal: open defecation is concentrated in the arid, low-stunting south, and improved water sources cluster in the dense highlands. They are reported unedited because dropping inconvenient coefficients is how spurious findings become durable ones — but neither should be read as a mechanism, and neither supports a WASH recommendation in either direction.

06

Validation, including what failed

Benchmarking holds

Aggregated to the 23 DHS reporting regions, the model agrees with the design-based direct estimate in 22 of 23, mean absolute difference 2.57 points. Ratio benchmark factors run 0.784 to 1.201 with a median of 0.967, so no region required a large correction.

Calibration: the beta-binomial earns its place

Four likelihoods were compared on the predictive distribution for a new cluster, using the randomised PIT of Czado, Gneiting & Held (2009) — the valid diagnostic for count data, where an ordinary PIT is not uniform even under a perfect model.

WAIC and DIC prefer the binomial with a nugget by roughly 20 units; leave-one-out log CPO and calibration prefer the beta-binomial. The tie was broken on purpose: this model exists to give intervals where the survey never went, so whether the new-cluster predictive distribution is honest outranks in-sample predictive density. Adding a nugget on top of the beta-binomial leaves it unidentified — nugget precision ~2000 with a credible interval spanning three orders of magnitude — so the parsimonious form was taken, with intra-cluster correlation ρ = 0.029.

The covariates do not improve prediction

Under spatial block cross-validation — contiguous 60 km tiles held out, so the model must extrapolate the distance it really faces — adding all earth-observation covariates makes out-of-sample prediction worse: RMSE 0.193 against 0.186 for the spatial field alone, correlation 0.287 against 0.350.

This does not contradict the R² result, and it must be reported beside it. The covariates are themselves smooth in space, so for interpolation the Matérn field already carries their information and the extra parameters cost precision. Their value here is explanatory, not predictive. Any claim that earth-observation covariates improve commune-level prediction accuracy is unsupported by this test.

Relatedly, in the surface model only precip_seasonality and frac_built have intervals excluding zero; elevation itself is −0.047 (−0.466 to 0.372). The 188 km field absorbs it. The explanatory evidence comes from the HAZ decomposition, not from the surface model's fixed effects.

Intervals are conservative, not exact

Every specification over-covers: about 97% of observations fall inside nominal 90% predictive intervals, and every PIT histogram is hump-shaped. The published commune intervals are therefore wider than nominal — the safer direction for a targeting product, but they should not be described as exact.

07

Where the children are

Prevalence and burden rank differently, and conflating them misallocates.

281 communes — 16.5% of the total — contain half of Madagascar's stunted children. The heaviest single burdens sit in peri-urban Analamanga and around Antsirabe, where high prevalence meets dense settlement. The highest-prevalence communes, by contrast, are small Ankaratra highland communes of a few thousand children each.

08

Limitations

  • This is mediation, not causal identification. A covariate that absorbs the highland penalty is a candidate mechanism, nothing more. Blocks are correlated, so entry order affects credit — which is why each block is also reported alone.
  • Altitude and temperature are one variable here (r = −0.90). No model on these data can separate cold stress from altitude per se.
  • Cattle density is a wealth proxy, not a diet measure. The highlands have more cattle and more stunting; Malagasy zebu function largely as stored wealth and ritual capital rather than as food for young children. H2 rests on the dietary-diversity variables, not on livestock.
  • Cluster-to-commune assignment is approximate. DHS coordinates are displaced up to 2 km (urban) or 5 km (rural), with 1% of rural clusters to 10 km. Displacement is restricted to admin2, so cluster-to-district is exact, but a cluster can cross a commune boundary. Covariates are therefore read over a displacement-matched buffer, never at the point.
  • Land cover enters the surface at commune resolution. Computing 10 m WorldCover fractions for every grid cell is not affordable, so within-commune variation in cropland and built-up area is left to the spatial field.
  • 45 communes are smaller than one 5 km grid cell and take the nearest cell's posterior draws. At a 188 km range the surface is effectively constant over those distances, but their estimates are borrowed rather than computed.
  • Region Ambatosoa cannot be reported separately. It exists in the 2025 adm1 layer but has no districts or communes in adm2/adm3; its territory sits under Analanjirofo. No population is lost.
  • The IHME external comparison has not been run — the Local Burden of Disease raster is not redistributable and is not present locally.
09

Reproducing this

The pipeline runs end to end from run_all.R in R 4.5.1. Boundaries, WorldPop and every earth-observation raster download automatically and cache; only the DHS microdata must be supplied, and it is gitignored under the DHS terms of use, which forbid redistribution.

Three boundary defects in the official 2025 layers are repaired explicitly, with an audit trail written to outputs/tables/: an incomplete 2021 Vatovavy–Fitovinany split, inconsistent Antananarivo arrondissement p-codes, and a commune adjacency graph in four components that an ICAR prior requires to be connected.

Night lights come from the harmonised DMSP–VIIRS series rather than NOAA's annual VNL V2, which now sits behind an account. Values are DMSP-like digital numbers on a 0–63 scale, not radiances, and they saturate over Antananarivo — usable as a rural gradient, not as a linear intensity measure.
